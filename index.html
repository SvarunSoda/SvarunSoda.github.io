About Me

Professional Summary

I am a recent M.S. Computer Science graduate from California State University, Northridge. My research centers on unconventional computational models, specifically oscillator-based temporal computing systems that encode and process analog values through phase relationships rather than binary logic. I am broadly interested in low-level systems, novel computational architectures, and the intersection of hardware and software. My academic work is complemented by industry experience in aerospace, astrophotonics, and medical devices.

Research Focus: Temporal Computing

My M.S. thesis, Phase-based Oscillatory Computation (PHOSCO), proposes a discrete-time analog computing framework where numerical values are stored as relative phase differences (Δφ) between pairs of concurrently-running oscillators. The work introduces oscillator pausing mechanics for value storage, derives four linear arithmetic operations from conditional pausing logic circuits, and validates the framework through a custom-built 6-oscillator prototype with full evaluation of storage longevity and operation precision.
